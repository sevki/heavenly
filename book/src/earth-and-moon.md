# Earth, Moon and Mars

The `heavenly::geo` crate connects body-fixed frames to inertial ones and
locates things on body surfaces.

This is the `heavenly::geo` module of the `heavenly` crate (cargo feature `geo`, on by default).

## Ellipsoids and geodetic coordinates

`Geodetic { latitude_rad, longitude_rad, height_m }` is defined relative to an
`Ellipsoid`. Latitude is measured along the ellipsoid normal and longitude is
east-positive. Built-in ellipsoids:

| Ellipsoid | a (m) | 1/f |
|-----------|-------|-----|
| `WGS84` | 6 378 137 | 298.257 223 563 |
| `GRS80` | 6 378 137 | 298.257 222 101 |
| `MOON` | 1 737 400 | sphere |
| `MARS` | 3 396 190 | ≈169.9 (b = 3 376 200 m) |

<figure class="hv-figure">
{{#heavenly-diagram geodetic}}
<figcaption>Geodetic latitude φ is measured along the ellipsoid normal, not from the centre; height h is along the same normal. Computed with <code>Geodetic</code> on an ellipsoid with exaggerated flattening.</figcaption>

`Geodetic::from_cartesian` uses Heikkinen's closed-form solution, with no
iteration. `Geodetic::enu_to_body_fixed` builds the transform for a local
east-north-up frame, which is how facility, building and antenna frames are
attached to a body.

## Earth orientation (ITRF ↔ GCRF)

<figure class="hv-figure">
{{#heavenly-diagram earth-orientation}}
<figcaption>The rotation from GCRS to ITRS, one factor per physical effect.</figcaption>
</figure>

`earth::register(&mut graph, eop)` adds the ITRF → GCRF edge. The model follows
the IERS 2010 CIO-based procedure:

* IAU 2006 frame bias and precession (Fukushima–Williams angles), in full;
* the 20 largest terms of IAU 2000B nutation;
* the CIO locator `s` and the TIO locator `s′`;
* the Earth Rotation Angle, from UT1;
* polar motion, from the supplied EOP.

## Earth orientation parameters (EOP)

The Earth's rotation is not predictable to the precision of the model above.
IERS measures it and publishes the differences as Earth orientation
parameters: polar motion (x, y) and UT1 − UTC. Without them, results are off
by up to ≈15 m (polar motion) and ≈400 m (UT1). This also affects SGP4
satellites, through TEME → ITRF.

`eop::EopTable` reads the two standard IERS files and implements
`EopProvider`, so you can pass it straight to `earth::register`:

```rust,ignore
let eop = EopTable::open("finals2000A.all")?;   // or eopc04.1962-now
earth::register(&mut frames, eop)?;
```

| File | From | Covers |
|------|------|--------|
| [`finals2000A.all`](https://maia.usno.navy.mil/ser7/finals2000A.all) | IERS Rapid Service (USNO) | 1973 to now, then about a year of **predictions** |
| [`eopc04.1962-now`](https://hpiers.obspm.fr/iers/eop/eopc04/eopc04.1962-now) | IERS Earth Orientation Centre (Paris) | the definitive series, 1962 (read from 1972) to a few weeks ago |

How the table is used:

* **Interpolation.** Values are given daily at 0h UTC and interpolated with
  four-point Lagrange polynomials, as IERS recommends. UT1 is interpolated
  as UT1 − TAI, which is continuous, so leap seconds are handled exactly.
* **Final vs rapid values.** Where finals2000A has the final (Bulletin B)
  values, they are used; otherwise the rapid (Bulletin A) ones.
* **Predictions.** `first_predicted()` says where the predicted values start.
* **No extrapolation.** Outside the table, lookups fail with
  `OutsideCoverage`; nothing is extrapolated.
* **Without std.** `EopTable::parse` takes the file's text, so it also works
  without std (it needs `alloc`).

For a fixed set of values, for example from a Bulletin A you read yourself,
use `EarthOrientation::from_bulletin`.

## IAU body models (Moon, Mars)

`iau::register(&mut graph)` adds `MOON_IAU → MOON_ICRF` and
`MARS_IAU → MARS_ICRF`. These use the IAU/WGCCRE rotation models, with
constants copied from NAIF's `pck00011.tpc`. Angular velocity includes the
pole motion and every periodic term.

These are cartographic conventions, not dynamical ephemerides. For metre-level
lunar work, use the DE440 `MOON_PA` / `MOON_ME` frames from
[`heavenly::spice`](spice.md) instead.

## Accuracy

Every number below is measured, not estimated. The validation tests check it
against values generated by independent implementations (SOFA via pyerfa, and
SPICE). The generator is `heavenly-geo/tests/reference/generate.py`.

| Quantity | Reference | Worst difference | At the surface |
|----------|-----------|------------------|----------------|
| Geodetic ↔ Cartesian (WGS84, −10 km to 40 000 km) | exact forward (SOFA `gd2gc`) | < 1 µm | — |
| ITRF ↔ GCRF, 1980–2050, same EOP | SOFA `c2t06a` (IAU 2006/2000A) | 7.6 mas | ≈ 0.24 m |
| `MOON_IAU`, 1960–2100 | SPICE `pxform`, pck00011 | 0.0005 mas | ≈ 4 µm |
| `MARS_IAU`, 1960–2100 | SPICE `pxform`, pck00011 | 0.01 mas | ≈ 0.2 mm |
| `MOON_IAU` vs DE440 `MOON_ME`, 1970–2049 | SPICE binary PCK | 18″ | ≈ 150 m |
| `MOON_IAU` vs DE440 `MOON_PA`, 1970–2049 | SPICE binary PCK | 116″ | ≈ 980 m |

Earth accuracy in practice depends on the EOP you supply. Using zero for
polar motion costs up to ≈15 m, and using zero for UT1 − UTC costs up to
≈400 m.

`EopTable` is checked against astropy's IERS readers
(`heavenly-geo/tests/reference/generate_eop.py`) on finals2000A and C04:

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Values on the tabulated days | identical |
| Between days, against astropy's linear interpolation | ≤ 29 µs UT1, ≤ 0.16 mas pole |
| Days left out of the table, predicted from their neighbours | Lagrange 39 µs and 0.27 mas; linear 110 µs and 0.45 mas |
| UT1 across the 2016-12-31 leap second | continuous |

29 µs of UT1 is about 1.3 cm at the equator, and 0.16 mas of pole position
is about 5 mm.

Angular velocities are checked against finite differences of the rotation.
The Earth model neglects precession, nutation and polar-motion rates, which
are below 1e-11 rad/s, or about 0.1 mm/s at the surface.
